What is a Brain Tumor

A brain tumor is an abnormal growth of cells within the skull—either primary (originating in the brain) or secondary/metastatic (spreading from another body part). The tumor can be benign (non-cancerous) or malignant (cancerous). Any space‑occupying lesion can harm the brain due to pressure buildup.

Types of Brain Tumors

  • Meningioma: The most common benign tumor, arising from the brain's membranes. Slow-growing and often cured with surgery. Represents ~37% of primary brain tumors and ~98% are benign.
  • Gliomas: Includes astrocytomas, oligodendrogliomas, and highly aggressive glioblastoma. Make up ~80% of malignant brain tumors and ~30% of all CNS tumors.
  • Pituitary Adenomas: Usually benign but may affect hormone balance and vision. Occur in approximately 17% of the population.
